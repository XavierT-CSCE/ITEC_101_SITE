My Ethics

My Core Four

Penetration Testing is considered a form of White Hat Hacking, which is a term used for a person who has authorization to attack software, systems, or infrastructure. With White Hat Hacking, there are four core ethical values that guide how penetration testers conduct their work.

Consent: Penetration testers must gain explicit permission from a higher-up or organization before performing any attacks or security assessments. Without authorization, testing could become illegal or unethical.

Boundaries: Penetration testers must follow established guidelines that define what systems can be tested, when testing may occur, and how the testing should be conducted. These boundaries prevent authorized testing from becoming unauthorized activity.

Confidentiality: Penetration testers must ensure that any information they collect remains confidential and out of public reach. Sensitive data and testing methods should only be disclosed to authorized personnel within the organization.

Compliance: Penetration testers must operate within legal and regulatory requirements, including privacy, data protection, and intellectual property laws. Following these regulations helps ensure that testing remains ethical and lawful.

Breaking Ethical Code

When it comes to not adhearing to these values, one of the major consequences is being fined or even imprisoned for breaking laws such as the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA). This act allows charges to be placed on an indiviual who gains unauthorized access to protected information or data via. computers. Another possible consequence is data loss or data leakage. If a pentester isn't careful with things such as disclosure or protection, they can possibly cause a dataleak, which is where massive amounts of user data is released to the public, putting others at risk.
